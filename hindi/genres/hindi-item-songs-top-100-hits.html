Iconic Bollywood Item Songs That Made History

Bollywood's item-song tradition did not begin with the modern label. Earlier Hindi films featured cabaret performances, dance interludes and special musical appearances. Over time, these sequences evolved into the standalone, star-led dance numbers audiences now commonly call item songs.

1950s–1970s: The cabaret era

  • Mera Naam Chin Chin Chu – Howrah Bridge (1958): Helen's performance, sung by Geeta Dutt, became one of the best-known examples of the cabaret style in Hindi cinema.
  • Piya Tu Ab To Aaja – Caravan (1971): Asha Bhosle's expressive vocals and Helen's performance made this a defining cabaret number of its era.
  • Mehbooba Mehbooba – Sholay (1975): The gypsy-style musical sequence, featuring Helen and sung by R. D. Burman, remains instantly recognisable.

1990s: The modern item-number identity takes shape

  • Choli Ke Peeche – Khalnayak (1993): Sung by Alka Yagnik and Ila Arun, the song became a major talking point and sparked public debate over its lyrics and presentation.
  • Hamma Hamma – Bombay (1995): A.R. Rahman's infectious composition and its distinctive dance sequence made it a hugely memorable 1990s film song. It is best described as an iconic dance number rather than a conventional standalone item appearance.
  • Chaiyya Chaiyya – Dil Se.. (1998): Shah Rukh Khan and Malaika Arora's performance on top of a moving train became one of Hindi cinema's most unforgettable song sequences. Choreographer Farah Khan has spoken about the unusual train-top shoot.

2000s: Star-powered special appearances

  • Khallas – Company (2002): Isha Koppikar's performance became a signature early-2000s dance number.
  • Kajra Re – Bunty Aur Babli (2005): A memorable on-screen combination of Aishwarya Rai, Amitabh Bachchan and Abhishek Bachchan helped make this song a pop-culture favourite.
  • Beedi Jalaile – Omkara (2006): Bipasha Basu's earthy performance, Gulzar's lyrics and Vishal Bhardwaj's music gave the song a distinctive rustic flavour.

2010s: Chartbusters and dance crazes

  • Munni Badnaam Hui – Dabangg (2010): Malaika Arora's performance became a nationwide hit and part of the much-discussed 2010 “Munni versus Sheila” moment.
  • Sheila Ki Jawani – Tees Maar Khan (2010): Katrina Kaif's dance number became the other defining side of that 2010 chartbuster rivalry.
  • Chikni Chameli – Agneepath (2012): Sung by Shreya Ghoshal, this high-energy Hindi adaptation of the Marathi song “Kombdi Palali” became a major dance favourite.
  • Baby Doll – Ragini MMS 2 (2014): Kanika Kapoor's Punjabi-flavoured vocals helped turn this into one of the decade's widely heard party songs.
  • Dilbar – Satyameva Jayate (2018): The recreated version of the 1999 song from Sirf Tum introduced the melody to a new generation, with Nora Fatehi's performance attracting wide attention.

2020s: Dance numbers keep evolving

  • Aaj Ki Raat – Stree 2 (2024): Tamannaah's special dance appearance became a prominent part of the film's music and promotion.
  • Tauba Tauba – Bad Newz (2024): Vicky Kaushal's dance performance showed how male-led, hook-step-driven film songs also shape today's dance trends. It is an adjacent dance hit, not a traditional item number.

Did you know?

  • The performers who popularised early cabaret numbers were often cast separately from a film's lead heroine; Helen became one of the era's best-known dance stars.
  • The phrase “item number” became especially prominent in the late 1990s and early 2000s, even though special dance performances had appeared in Hindi cinema much earlier.
  • Modern item numbers increasingly became promotional events in their own right, with a recognisable hook step, a star appearance and a music video-style presentation.
